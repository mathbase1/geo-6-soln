This list uses the same rules: seven different digits from 1–9, and two two-digit whole numbers on the right. It includes totals larger than 99.

What exactly has been counted?

For the reduced list, the bracket digits are in increasing order and the smaller right-side number is first. Each accepted equation generates four ordered box fillings by independently reversing those two additions.

The transfer generator computes N from the left side, then Q and r from division by 2. It tests every whole k from 0 to the smaller of Q − 10 and 99 − Q − r. It retains only candidates whose seven displayed digits are different and nonzero.

The independent verifier does something else: it places seven different digits into the seven boxes in every possible order, then compares the two arithmetic values. It tests 9 × 8 × 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 181,440 arrangements. Agreement between the two accepted sets checks the count without trusting the transfer formula.
